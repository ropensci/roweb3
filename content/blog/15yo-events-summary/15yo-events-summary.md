In June 2026, rOpenSci marked its 15th anniversary with tree community celebration events. This post captures the reflections and ideas shared by participants across the two special gatherings named **"rOpenSci Community Celebration: Then, Now & Next"** that were held to accomodate differents time zones.

This post captures the events dinamic and the reflections and ideas shared by participants across both gatherings.

## How the Events Worked

Our goal was to create a space for people to have conversations, getting to know each other and also reflect on rOpenSci's present and future. 

After a welcome and a brief introduction, participants joined three successive breakout rounds of 15 minutes each, one for each theme: the **past**, the **present**, and the **future**. Between rounds, attendees took part in a **Scavenger Hunt** (a regular feature of our co-working sessions), finding something in their physical surroundings that matched a given prompt.

The Scavenger Hunt is a fun moment together, and we also use it as a natural prompt for introductions at the start of the next breakout room — "what did you find?" becomes a nice and easy opening for conversations.

As we used in our community calls, workshops and co-woring sessions, a shared document served as the place everyone could take notes in real time, add thoughts, and keep contributing for a couple of days after the event ended. Participants named this _"process is product"_ approach, caring about *how* things happen, not just *what* happens, as one of rOpenSci's defining traits.


## Round 1 - The Past: What Made rOpenSci Special?

The first breakout asked participants how they first connected with rOpenSci and what their favourite memory was.

Several people traced their connection back to a talk about rOpenSci or to a personal introduction from a community member who made them feel welcome from the start. 

The **unconferences** were remembered with particular warmth and excitement: the chance to sit next to people, see how they work, talk about R, and be part of something as it was taking shape. _"Meeting my heroes"_, as one participant put it.

Other things people named as favourites:

- **The peer-review process**: praised by editors, reviewers and authors alike, both for the quality of the process and for the connections it created.

> Being invited to write a blog post after a first package review It made me feel very cool and a valued part of the community."

- **The dev guide**: because is a detailed and evolving documentation with knowledge that isn't easily found elsewhere.

- **The Champions Program** and **the mini-hackathon**, because are spaces that provide friendly and guided entry points for open source contributors and R package developers.

- **Co-working sessions** are a place were you can find "your people," a place where you don't need to explain what R is.

> It's a satisfying community 'distraction/procrastination' from our normal jobs, that keeps us from being lonely and brings us together from different worlds to work on something that's benefitting people."

## Round 2 - The Present: What Does rOpenSci Do Well?

The second round invited reflection on what rOpenSci does well today and what participants want to get more involved in.

The most common answer was **creating a welcoming space**.



> You can jump into a role and the community will support you. If there is anything you want to do, start doing it and you will get the support you need."


## Round 3 - The Future: What Comes Next?

The third round asked what participants would like to see rOpenSci do next, and what ideas from other communities could be worth adopting or partnering on.
